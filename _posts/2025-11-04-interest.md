# Research plans

The choice among the SSL Assumptions can lead to conceptually different learning algorithms. Selecting correct assumptions for a given problem can deliver high predictive accuracy, whereas the wrong ones can produce worse results than fully supervised algorithms. I’m keen on unifying SSL Assumptions in a single algorithm, which involves finding automatic criteria for selecting SSL Assumptions. My first attempt in this sense is to use Bayesian Learning and Evolutionary Algorithms to find the correct relationship between assumptions and data. This line of work can produce strong collaborations between the Natural Computation and Machine Learning Groups at the SoCS. Prof. Xin Yao and Dr. Leandro Minku are researchers that can help this research to make great strides. 
I’m keen on unifying SSL Assumptions analytically. A first investigation in this line would be to instantiate the Bayesian Information Criterion to SSL. This could potentially involve researchers with expertise in Learning Theory, for instance, Prof. Ata Kaban and Prof. Peter Tino from the Machine Learning Group. There are many possible applications for this research. In order to continue exploring an area close to industry, we could apply this research to Software Engineering problems, in which Dr. Leandro Minku is an expert.
My current research with Dr. Leandro Minku proposes to merge SSL and Online Learning. This line of research has the potential to lead to fruitful collaborations in various interesting approaches to solve relevant problems.
There has been an increasing interest in Evolutionary Algorithms for the training of Deep Neural Networks due to the difficulties of traditional gradient-based training algorithms in finding proper weights in the presence of many hidden layers. In particular, many researchers study the training of Deep Neural Networks as a multiobjective problem with accuracy and complexity as objectives. They usually employ Multiobjective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEA) to optimize the entire network architecture and train its weights. I intend to develop Deep SSL as a new line of research. The first investigation would be dealing with Deep SSL as a multiobjective problem. Each SSL algorithm has in its design a set of assumptions on the relationship between unlabelled data and targets. This fact leads to very different algorithms and the choice of the right method for a given problem becomes challenging. Unlike the aforementioned supervised deep learning approaches, I will focus on using MOEAs to optimize such relationships between data and Deep SSL design in a unifying algorithm that finds both architecture and weights of Neural Networks. Such an investigation presents potential opportunities to work with Prof. Xin Yao, Dr. Leandro Minku, Prof. Ata Kaban and Prof. Peter Tino.
My work in Natural Language Processing started with [7]. To further expand our research in that area, I also intend to research Natural Language Processing and Deep Learning in the context of Software Engineering. Investigating Deep Learning techniques to perform estimations during Software Development with textual data from early documents might bring improvements in management effectiveness and decision making. In addition, this line of research can bring the Natural Language Processing, Artificial Intelligence and Software Engineering research groups to work together. More specifically, it can be in collaboration with Dr. Mark Lee, Dr. Leandro Minku and Dr. Rami Bahsoon.
The SoCS is the ideal institution for the development of my Lecturer activities as it has been shown in the last few years. I was able to publish key works in my area of expertise alongside Prof. Xin Yao and Dr. Huanhuan Chen. This link is quite strong to this date as I’m collaborating with Dr. Leandro Minku in a very rich research area. My research skills and research methodology have been greatly influenced by the SoCS. My line of research strongly coincides with the area of the Machine Learning Group as shown above. And my publications show that this relationship is productive. I believe that the above experience and research plans are a strong fit to the person specification of this position.
