## Early research

I’m a Lecturer and Researcher at the Federal Rural University of Pernambuco (UFRPE). In 2020, I was also a Research Fellow at the University of Birmingham (UoB). My main area of expertise is Artificial Intelligence, more specifically, Machine Learning (ML) with strong connections to Data Sciences. I have worked extensively in Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL), Ensemble Learning and Neural Networks. SSL algorithms are able to deliver better learning performance than supervised methods by learning from both labelled and unlabelled data. In Data Sciences, labelling instances is often expensive, whereas unlabelled data is typically cheap and abundant, making SSL valuable. Such algorithms have several applications, for example, text classification, credit card approval, etc. I have also worked in Natural Language Processing and Deep Learning in the context of effort estimation for software corrections. In the School of Computer Science (SoCS) at the UoB, I recently collaborated as a research fellow with Dr. Leandro Minku in the project “Stable Prediction of Defect-Inducing Software Changes” at the SoCS to propose ML solutions to software defects in non-stationary environments. Previously, I also worked with Dr. Xin Yao and Dr. Huanhuan Chen (a former research fellow at the SoCS). Currently, I’m investigating learning approaches to the automatic selection of SSL Assumptions and online SSL.
The SoCS has provided the ideal environment for the development of my research in ML. I have been collaborating with staff members since the beginning of my Ph.D. course. Important contributions in ML emerged from these collaborations. The first of these key works in SSL was published in the IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems (TNNLS) - I’m attaching a separate document with a list of my publications, please refer to this list when possible. This work stemmed from a productive collaboration with Prof. Xin Yao and Dr. Huanhuan Chen during my Ph.D. course. We proposed the first algorithm to explicitly employ cluster structure in the training of multiclass neural networks in order to learn from unlabelled data. This learning method uses soft clustering in a novel regularized training to make a neural network learn from unlabelled data for multiclass classification. We could demonstrate that our approach delivers higher learning performance than both supervised and semi-supervised methods when data have meaningful cluster structures. We continued this line of research by proposing an ensemble of neural networks that is trained as a single SSL machine. It employs unlabelled data in the training of the ensemble as well as its semi-supervised base learners. Such an algorithm allows base classifiers to overcome potential incorrect label estimates for unlabeled data. It is effective and stable in the presence of overlapping classes and scarce labeled points in dense regions, which is often the case in several real-world applications. Such an algorithm delivered higher predictive accuracy than state-of-the-art cluster-based semi-supervised methods. Our latest key contribution to ML was published. This study was also a collaboration with Prof. Xin Yao and Dr. Huanhuan Chen published in TNNLS. SSL algorithms assume that they have access to massive amounts of unlabelled data and few labelled instances. This fact makes SSL a relevant alternative for learning systems in Big Data. However, there was not a major concern about time and memory requirements in SSL literature. This fact led to severe resources bottlenecks in Big Data applications. To solve such a severe limitation of SSL, we proposed a neural network ensemble that is able to deal with large-scale learning in the context of multiclass semi-supervised classification. We intended to create an efficient SSL classifier that could handle Big Data applications. To accomplish such a goal, we tackled an important limitation of semi-supervised algorithms: high computational time and memory when dealing with typical amounts of unlabelled data. We performed a comprehensive study on computational and memory complexities of existing algorithms as well as proposed a SSL neural network ensemble classifier that could outperform the other methods in terms of both computational time and memory. With a lower computational cost, this algorithm can match the generalization accuracy or even outperform all other existing methods.
I also pursue research in Deep Learning in order to solve Natural Language Processing and Software Engineering problems. In this work, I proposed the use of different autoencoders to perform feature extraction for text classification. This approach contributed to the improvement of effort estimation in early stages of software development, in which there are only textual documents available as a basis for estimations.
